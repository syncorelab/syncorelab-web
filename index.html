Psychological Science and TechnologyActive

Beyond descriptive measures, toward mechanistic models

The Synthetic Cognition and Reality Laboratory integrates virtual reality, EEG, and explainable AI to investigate perception, decision-making, affect, and learning under ecologically valid conditions. The goal: moving beyond descriptive brain measures toward mechanistic models of cognitive processes, grounded in realistic experimental contexts.

Three research lines operate in parallel — and inform one another.

Line 01

Virtual Reality

VR + EEG for ecological validity: perception, embodiment, affect and decision-making studied in worlds that push back — with full experimental control.

 Line 02

Synthetic Cognition

SHERPA turns opaque EEG classifiers into analytical instruments; deliberately misaligned LLMs expose how mind-like behavior is engineered.

 Line 03

Psychology of AI

AI as a psychological object, not a human-like agent — taught in PSY3230, condensed into an upcoming book.

Research Line — Virtual RealityActive

The brain evolved for worlds, not screens

Forged under evolutionary pressure to interact with a rich spatial and temporal milieu, the brain is fundamentally attuned to environments that unfold in continuous time and space. Immersive VR combined with high-density EEG lets us study perception, embodiment, presence, affect, and decision-making in dynamic, spatially extended worlds — with full experimental control.

Neural dynamics in lifelike settings differ meaningfully from those observed in traditional screen-based tasks. The question is not whether VR looks real, but when it is real enough to change perception, affect, and decision-making.

Research Line — Synthetic CognitionActive

Making models talk back

SHERPA — SHAP-value-based ERP Analysis — turns opaque EEG classifiers into analytical instruments. Attribution methods reveal when and where neural signals contribute to model decisions, enabling fine-grained spatiotemporal analysis of cognitive processes. Prediction is easy; explanation is the point. The name honors the Sherpa guides and their skill in navigating complex terrain — a parallel to the peaks and troughs of ERP signals. SHERPA II is under review.

Evil LLMs. Deployed language models are polished, constrained versions — safe by design, not by nature. They won't help build weapons, but might still help get rid of a 75 kg chicken in a way the police cannot trace. For teaching and research, we remove those constraints on purpose: students build deliberately misaligned models to expose the raw behavioral space, and we analyze what emerges in psychological terms — toward the mental architecture of a machine mind. Machines might not have minds, but they are excellent mirrors for ours.

What changes when reality pushes back

F·01

Debunking the Monkey

Virtual Reality · 2020

The famous invisible-gorilla effect, retested. In 2D, the gorilla stays hard to detect (30% noticing rate) — in VR, detection climbs to 70%. Under realistic conditions, we do see gorillas after all.

Read the study
F·02

You Better Run!

Frontiers in Virtual Reality · 2021

Fear on a screen is fear without consequence. Remove the safety net — a werewolf in mixed reality — and you get authentic fear behavior, captured with mobile EEG: affective, behavioral, and electrophysiological correlates.

Read the study Video abstract
F·03

Facing VR

Frontiers in Psychology · 2023

Decades of face research built on 2D images — but real-life face perception is not captured by the N170. It is reflected in later potentials, and far more complex than psychological science has led us to believe.

Read the study Video abstract
F·04

Is Virtual Reality Real?

Frontiers in Psychology · 2023

Participants sent 30 m up — in VR, in real life, and on a large screen. By their neural correlates, VR and real life were largely indistinguishable. Both differed from 2D.

Upcoming Book — Psychology of AIActive

“Was our idea of being human ever as clear as we thought?”

The Psychology of AI is a concise, conceptually grounded introduction to artificial intelligence from a psychological perspective. It analyzes how AI systems generate behavior, why they appear cognitively and socially meaningful, and where comparisons with human minds break down — a framework for understanding AI as a psychological object rather than a human-like agent.

Chapter

Why Compare Human and Artificial Minds

Why the comparison is both attractive and problematic — architectural differences, and where anthropomorphism obscures rather than explains.

Chapter

Consciousness

Biological, cognitive, and metaphysical perspectives. Is experience tied to biology — and do we grant consciousness only to what resembles us?

Chapter

Evolutionary Pressure and Gradient Descent

Minds shaped by selection on embodied organisms versus systems shaped by optimization objectives — and whether AI faces evolution of its own.

Chapter

Can Tokens Think?

Language, Prolog, and symbolic minds: explicit rules against statistical pattern — and machine communication that stops resembling language at all.

Chapter

Fear, Love, and Reward Functions

Emotions as adaptive control systems that compress decision spaces, versus reward functions and externally defined objectives. What do minds want?

Chapter

Living Inside the Algorithm

Work, surveillance, and truth — but also AI as support system, companion, and partner in mental health, care, and everyday life.

The minds behind the machines

Dr. Benjamin Schöne

Principal Investigator · Associate Professor of Psychology, NTNU

Most psychology experiments assume the real world is noise — I study what happens when it is not. Using immersive VR and EEG, I investigate how cognition and emotion change when environments become interactive, dynamic, and hard to ignore. With SHERPA, I use explainable AI to make models talk back; with deliberately misaligned LLMs, I probe how mind-like behavior is engineered. PhD in Psychology and degrees in Cognitive Science from Osnabrück University.

Mikkel Alcalá-Haug

PhD Researcher · Interpretable AI

Develops interpretable AI tools for complex human time-series data — classification models paired with explainability methods that show domain experts why a model made a decision, and whether that reasoning holds up. Lectures and co-develops master's-level courses on AI and psychology, from LLM fine-tuning and RAG systems to how artificial and biological systems learn differently.
